PART 3: FOUR TIERS. THAT'S IT.
Duke sits back down with the client. A whiteboard marker appears in his hand. Nobody saw him pick it up.
DUKE Classification means applying labels to your data that describe how sensitive it is and how it should be handled. Simple concept. Four tiers is usually right. Here's what we recommend:
TIER WHAT IT COVERS WHAT HAPPENS IF IT LEAKS
PUBLIC Press releases, published reports, public meeting minutes Nothing. That's the point.
INTERNAL Org charts, internal memos, procedure documents Awkward, but not catastrophic.
CONFIDENTIAL Employee records, financial data, legal correspondence, CJIS-adjacent info Real liability. Regulatory exposure. Bad headlines.
RESTRICTED PHI, SSNs, criminal justice data, specific regulatory mandates The data that keeps your compliance officer up at night.
DUKE DILLINGHAM
DUKE These tiers aren't revolutionary. What's revolutionary is making them operational — attached to every document, enforced by policy, and understood by every person who touches data.
DUDE DILIGENCE
DUDE Revolutionary? Duke, labels on documents is literally the bare minimum. We're not curing diseases here. We're putting name tags on files that contain Social Security numbers. The fact that this qualifies as "revolutionary" should concern everyone.

PART 6: THE PLAYBOOK
DUKE DILLINGHAM
DUKE If you're reading this and thinking "we should probably do this," here's the practical path:
  1. Define your tiers. Four is usually right. Align to your regulatory obligations, not someone else's template.
  2. Start with manual labeling in one department. Learn from their workflow before you scale.
  3. Deploy Purview sensitivity labels in M365. Even without automation, behavior changes immediately.
  4. Layer in automated classification with Varonis. Find what humans missed.
  5. Tie labels to DLP policies. A label without a policy is a suggestion. A label with a policy is a guardrail.
  6. Extend to AI governance. Now you have enforceable AI use policies — not just a document nobody reads.

DUDE Duke's being diplomatic. Let me be direct.
