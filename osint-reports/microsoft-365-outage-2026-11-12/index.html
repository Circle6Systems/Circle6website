Flash Briefing: Microsoft 365 Outage

Global · Thursday-Friday, November 12-13, 2026 · Flash Briefing v1

A failure in Exchange Online, Microsoft's hosted email service, disrupted Outlook and about 20 dependent Microsoft 365 services worldwide for more than a day. Microsoft attributes it to "an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced following a recent network change," and says the outage was "not related to cyber or AI / agents." This was not a general outage of the Office web apps: core Teams calls and chat kept working. No post-incident report has been published.

Bottom Line Up Front

  • The event is confirmed, and it was not an "Office online" outage. The failure centered on Exchange Online (Outlook and email). Features of about 20 other Microsoft 365 services that depend on Exchange were affected, including Copilot, Teams calendar functions, the Defender and Purview portals, Intune, and Microsoft Graph [2, 5].
  • Core Teams calls, meetings, and chat were not affected, and files stored directly in SharePoint Online stayed accessible, according to Microsoft [2].
  • Official cause, so far: "This was a technical issue not related to cyber or AI / agents. The incident was introduced by an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced following a recent network change." [39, 49] No post-incident report has been published; Microsoft's root-cause field read "N/A" [5].
  • Cyberattack: Microsoft has repeatedly said it has no evidence of malicious activity [5, 45]; Australia's Home Affairs Minister said Microsoft does not believe it is dealing with a cyber event [55]. We assess a cyberattack is very unlikely (moderate confidence). No independent government finding has been published.
  • Start time revised. Microsoft's incident records first showed starts of 11:17 AM ET and 1:26 PM ET on November 12. A public mirror of those records, captured at 04:32 UTC on November 14, showed both revised to 5:05 AM ET (10:05 UTC), without explanation [74].
  • FAA ground stop: a Microsoft spokesperson said the company has "no indication" the November 13 FAA ground stop is related [37, 42]. We assess a link is very unlikely (moderate confidence).
  • Watch for outage-themed phishing. The UK NCSC warned of scams impersonating IT help desks or Microsoft [51]. No security vendor or government source has confirmed a campaign.

02 · Microsoft Revised Its Records

Start times moved six to eight hours earlier

When collected (about 04:00 UTC, November 14), a public mirror of Microsoft's incident records showed the Exchange incident (EX1550543) starting at 11:17 AM ET (16:17 UTC) and the wider incident (MO1550821) at 1:26 PM ET (18:26 UTC) on November 12; press also reported the 11:17 AM time [5, 39]. A later capture of the same mirror at 04:32 UTC showed both start times revised to 5:05 AM ET (10:05 UTC): about 6 hours earlier for EX1550543 and about 8.5 hours earlier for MO1550821. The Exchange incident was marked "service restored" (record updated 04:12 UTC) with no end time; the wider incident remained degraded (record updated 04:07 UTC). Root cause still read "N/A" [74].

Microsoft has not explained the revision, or said whether 5:05 AM ET marks customer impact or an earlier internal trigger. The revised time matches the start on Microsoft's consumer status feed [4]. A separate Teams messaging incident began at 9:45 AM ET, but Microsoft has not linked it [58]; it does not corroborate a 5:05 AM start.

Microsoft, on cause (Service Health, 12:57 PM ET / 17:57 UTC, November 13)

"We are seeing broad recovery and stability across our services. This was a technical issue not related to cyber or AI / agents. The incident was introduced by an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced following a recent network change. We have addressed the underlying issue and continue to restore service to remaining customers and regions." [5, 49]

05 · Credible Reporting Beyond Official Statements

  • Azure network configuration (single source): The Verge reported that, according to a senior correspondent's sources, the outage "is linked to Azure VNet config changes" [60]. Microsoft has not confirmed this detail; its own statement names a network capacity upgrade and network change, so the two agree only at the category level.
  • Traffic data: Kentik data showed "a dramatic drop in traffic to Microsoft Exchange Online" [39], with traffic returning "after about 18 hours of downtime" [61].
  • Downdetector (user reports, not measurements): reports from 74 countries and territories within about an hour; Outlook reports "peaked at more than 2,000 times their normal level" [36]; up to 19,650 reports per minute across five services [39].
  • Expert view: Ciaran Martin, former head of the UK NCSC, told NBC the disruption "looks like being one of the biggest outages of all time" [39].
  • Workforce speculation, countered: AP: "Microsoft has not tied this week's outage to recent job reductions at the company." [36]
Conflicts we could not resolve
  • USA Today says Microsoft "first announced" issues at 10:40 a.m. ET on November 12 [46]; the separate Teams incident may explain this, but that is not confirmed.
  • USA Today says the service health page showed all services "operational" at 3 p.m. ET on November 13 [46]; the enterprise status page still showed "Service degradation" at 04:02 UTC November 14 [1]. The USA Today line may refer to the consumer view.

07 · Key Assessments

KA-1: This was an Exchange Online failure whose effects spread to dependent Microsoft 365 features, not a general failure of the Office web apps. Almost certain; high confidence.
High confidence

Microsoft's incident text ("Exchange is the primarily impacted service") and dependency list [5, 2]. The only Word for the web issue was a separate advisory with its own cause [5].

KA-2: The proximate trigger was an internal Microsoft network change interacting with an earlier capacity upgrade, causing servers to fail health checks and cascade under load. Very likely; moderate confidence.
Moderate confidence

Basis: Microsoft's statements over about 26 hours [5]. Limits: one first-party source; no PIR; Microsoft first said it had found no correlating change. The cascade description (failed servers shift load onto the rest, which then fail) is our analysis of Microsoft's statements, not a Microsoft finding.

KA-3: A cyberattack caused the outage. Very unlikely; moderate confidence.
Moderate confidence

Basis: repeated Microsoft statements [5]; the Australian minister's account after speaking with the national cyber coordinator [55]. Limits: no independent government finding; CISA declined comment [51]; the investigation is not complete.

KA-4: AI agents or AI-generated changes caused the outage. Unlikely; low confidence.
Low confidence

Basis: Microsoft's explicit denial [5]. Limits: the denial is first-party, and the change mechanism is not yet published.

KA-5: The outage is linked to the FAA ground stop on November 13. Very unlikely; moderate confidence.
Moderate confidence

Basis: Microsoft's spokesperson and the DHS Secretary [37, 42, 44]. See our FAA ground stop briefing.

KA-6: Some customer impact began before the originally published 11:17 AM ET start. Roughly even chance; low confidence.
Low confidence

Fact, not assessment: Microsoft's records, as captured from a public mirror, now date both incidents to 5:05 AM ET (10:05 UTC) [74]. Whether that time marks customer impact is the open question. Basis: the revised time matches Microsoft's consumer feed start [4], and USA Today reported a first Microsoft announcement at 10:40 AM ET [46]. Limits: Microsoft has not said what the revised time marks; the consumer event may be separate; the USA Today time is single-outlet.

KA-7: Microsoft declares both incidents resolved by November 16. Likely; moderate confidence.
Moderate confidence

"Continued recovery" and the "majority" of scenarios addressed at 02:13 UTC November 14 [5]; the Exchange incident was marked "service restored" at 04:12 UTC, but the wider incident remained degraded and neither had an end time [5].

KA-8: Microsoft publishes a post-incident report within two weeks. Likely; low confidence.
Low confidence

Microsoft and its executive promised one "shortly" [43, 5]. Limits: Microsoft's published 72-hour and 14-day review timelines are for Azure [11]; we found no equivalent Microsoft 365 commitment.

KA-9: Outage-themed phishing and help-desk impersonation rise over the next 72 hours. Likely; moderate confidence.
Moderate confidence

Basis: the UK NCSC warning [51] and forum reports [69]. Limits: no confirmed campaign.

KA-10: US congressional scrutiny continues. Likely; moderate confidence.
Moderate confidence

Basis: the House Homeland Security Chair's inquiry [51] and Rep. Gottheimer's request [47].

11 · Actions for Organizations That Depend on Microsoft 365

  1. Check your own tenant. In Service Health, look for EX1550543 and MO1550821, and OO1550244 if you use Word for the web. If the admin center will not load, use status.cloud.microsoft [1, 13].
  2. Poll status with a fallback. The Microsoft Graph service communications API exposes Service Health [14], but Graph calls that touch Exchange data may time out during this incident [2]; keep a non-Graph fallback.
  3. Review the security gap. Defender for Office 365 policy evaluation, Purview DLP and labeling, and Defender portal pages were degraded [2]. Once stable, review alerts, DLP actions, and audit searches for November 12-14.
  4. Recover time-critical inbound mail. Microsoft extended the email timeout to 48 hours [5]. Confirm receipt where a lost message causes harm (court filings, protection orders, clinical referrals) [18]. Do not mass-resend [30].
  5. Out-of-band communications. Keep a staff and customer contact path that does not depend on Microsoft 365, and test it. As one cybersecurity professor told AP, "A continuity plan that relies on the same platform that has just failed is not a plan." [36]
  6. Phishing (precaution, not an observed campaign). Warn staff about "restore your messages" and "verify your account" emails and fake help-desk calls citing the real incident numbers. Check recovery status only through the admin center or status page, never a link in an email.
  7. Emergency access. Confirm break-glass administrator accounts work and are not tied to a single service dependency [16]. Hygiene only; no fault was reported here.
  8. Service credits. Cloud Solution Provider customers claim through their partner [15]; direct customers follow the SLA [12].
