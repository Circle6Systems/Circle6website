Flash Briefing: FAA Nationwide Ground Stop

United States National Airspace System · Friday, November 13, 2026 · Flash Briefing v1

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) halted departures at every U.S. airport for about four hours and 22 minutes after a communications failure hit multiple air route traffic control centers. Officials blame aging, overloaded telecom equipment and say they have seen "zero evidence" of a cyberattack, but have not ruled one out. Aircraft already in the air kept flying: the order grounded departures, not "all flights."

Bottom Line Up Front

  • The event is confirmed. The FAA command center ordered a national ground stop of all departing traffic at all U.S. airports, effective 3:53 PM ET (20:53 UTC) on Friday, November 13 [2]. Federal officials said it was lifted at 8:15 PM ET (01:15 UTC, November 14) [15, 28].
  • "All flights grounded" is overstated. The order stopped departures. More than 3,500 aircraft already airborne continued and landed; medevac, life-saving cargo, law-enforcement, intra-Alaska, and Hawaii inter-island flights continued; some international departures were released [2, 15, 23, 25].
  • Official cause so far: a communications failure. The command center advisory reads "REASON: COMMUNICATIONS FAILURE" [3]. The Transportation Secretary blamed old, overloaded telecom routers and switches [26]. No root-cause finding has been published.
  • Cyberattack: officials say they have seen "zero evidence" but have not ruled it out [17, 27]. We assess a cyberattack is unlikely (moderate confidence).
  • Reported cause: CBS News, citing multiple unnamed sources, reported that an update to a contractor-operated FAA communications network caused a reboot, a crash, and the outage [28]. Single-source; not confirmed by the FAA.
  • Outlook: disruption will very likely continue through Saturday, November 14, and likely into Sunday, November 15.

01 · What the Order Did, and What It Did Not

The official order

Command center Advisory 111 (signed 3:54 PM ET / 20:54 UTC): "ALL AIRPORTS ARE GROUND STOPPED. ALL DEPARTING TRAFFIC SHOULD REMAIN GROUND STOPPED UNTIL FURTHER NOTICE. INBOUND INTERNATIONAL FLIGHTS WILL BE MANAGED ON A CASE BY CASE BASIS." [2]

What continued during the stop

  • Airborne aircraft continued to their destinations: more than 3,500 airborne at the stop, fewer than 400 by 8:00 PM ET (01:00 UTC) [15].
  • Exempt flights: medevac, life-saving cargo, and law-enforcement flights [15].
  • Alaska and Hawaii: flights within Alaska and Hawaii inter-island flights were not affected; West Coast to Hawaii flights continued [23].
  • International: some departures to Europe and across the Pacific were released [25, 26, 36].

03 · Official Statements

FAA
  • Written statement (dated November 13, no clock time): "The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop for all U.S. airports following communication issues impacting multiple Air Route Traffic Control Centers. FAA technicians are investigating the issue." [1]
  • Advisory 120: "REASON: COMMUNICATIONS FAILURE", the only cause wording from the FAA found so far [3]. Advisory 111: "EXTRAORDINARY CIRCUMSTANCES AND FOR REASONS OF SAFETY" [2].
Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy
  • "We have TDM telecoms, so this is analog telecom. Our switches, our routers, actually got overloaded with data" [26].
  • "We didn't lose all telecom, we didn't lose all radar." [26]
  • On funding: "the president's asked for $30 billion. I want $50 billion." [27]
  • Discrepancy: his post and the White House say "all" en route centers were affected [12, 18]; the FAA statement says "multiple" [1].
DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin
  • "We have seen zero evidence that this was a cyber attack or an SI attack" (DHS on X; the statement does not define "SI") [17].
  • "It appears that this was mechanically driven. However, we're still looking, so we're not going to rule that out." [32]
  • Earlier, asked about a cyber or AI intrusion: "That's not known." [29]
  • On the FAA's new AI-powered traffic tool, called SMART: "We're looking at that." [29]
  • The ground stop was not linked to this week's Microsoft outage (CNN's paraphrase) [27].
  • Single-source: only USA Today reports him saying the President declared the air traffic control failures "a national emergency" [31]. No such document was found on the White House briefings page at 9:22 PM ET (02:22 UTC).
Congress, and third-party statements (via AP)
  • House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee leaders: "The Committee will thoroughly review the issues that led to this nationwide ground stop." [20]
  • Senate Commerce Committee majority: the incident "highlights the need to prioritize modernizing ARTCCs" [19].
  • Microsoft: "no indication" the ground stop is related to its service disruption on Thursday, November 12 [25].
  • The developer of the SMART tool: the outage was "not based on or a result of the SMART system" [25].
  • National Weather Service: its radars were "operating normally" [25].
  • Checked at 9:13-9:41 PM ET (02:13-02:41 UTC): no FAA-related item on the CISA news page or the NTSB newsroom. FBI, DOT Inspector General, and NTSB social media accounts could not be checked (HTTP 403 or 404). This is a point-in-time absence, not evidence of silence.

04 · Credible Reporting Beyond Official Statements

Update-and-reboot account (CBS News, single source)
  • Multiple unnamed sources told CBS that an update to the private communications network connecting the air traffic control system caused it to reboot, crash, and lose communications [28].
  • CBS named the network's contractor. We do not: no official source has named it or assigned it responsibility.
  • Outlets repeating the CBS account are not independent corroboration [39, 40]. The FAA has not confirmed it [39].
  • A Bloomberg headline reads "US Says Software Patch Overwhelmed Air Control System"; the article was not accessible to us. Lead only.
Cascade (The Air Current, single source)
  • Outages began at the Chicago en route center, with Boston, Washington, New York, and Minneapolis "verifying they had problems as well in rapid succession" [36].
  • A carrier dispatcher was told the outage was affecting 10 en route centers "and growing" [36].
Other reporting
  • NBC: officials from multiple agencies said there was no indication of malicious activity, cautioning it was early [26].
  • AP: the communications problems "don't appear to be linked" to the FAA's new AI-powered system [25].

06 · Key Assessments

KA-1: Departures were halted nationwide from 3:53 PM ET to about 8:15 PM ET. Almost certain; high confidence.
High confidence

Basis: the advisory text, the FAA statement, official posts, and more than ten outlets [2, 1, 15, 25-37]. The lift time rests on officials and press; no command center cancellation advisory was captured.

KA-2: A failure in FAA air traffic communications across multiple en route centers was the proximate trigger. Very likely; high confidence on the category.
High confidence

Unresolved: whether all centers were affected. Officials say "all" [12, 18], the FAA says "multiple" [1], and The Air Current's source said "10 ... and growing" [36].

KA-3: The disruption began in the Chicago area before the 3:36 PM ET onset officials cited. Likely; moderate confidence.
Moderate confidence

Basis: FAA advisories for O'Hare ("COMMS ISSUES" from 3:10 PM ET), Midway, and the Chicago center [5, 6, 7], plus The Air Current [36]. Alternative: a local Chicago fault preceded a separate multi-center failure.

KA-4: The specific failure mechanism is undetermined. Low confidence on any single mechanism.
Low confidence

The officials' account (legacy routers overloaded; new technology on an old framework) [26, 25] and the CBS account (a network update triggered a reboot and crash) [28] may be compatible. Neither appears in an FAA written finding.

KA-5: A cyberattack caused the outage. Unlikely; moderate confidence.
Moderate confidence

Senior officials report no indicators and no claim of responsibility [17, 32, 26]. Limits: officials are "still looking", no CISA statement was found, FBI channels could not be checked, and the investigation is early.

KA-6: The SMART AI tool caused the outage. Unlikely; low confidence.
Low confidence

Basis: AP and the telecom-layer accounts [25, 26, 28]. Limits: officials are reviewing it, its rollout timing coincides, and the developer's denial comes from an interested party.

KA-7: The outage is linked to the Microsoft outage. Very unlikely; moderate confidence.
Moderate confidence

Microsoft, the DHS Secretary, and multi-agency officials all say there is no link [25, 27, 26].

KA-8: Flight disruption continues through Saturday, November 14. Very likely; likely into Sunday. Moderate confidence.
Moderate confidence

Carriers expect recovery "through the weekend" and disruption "into tomorrow" [23, 25]; new carrier-specific stops appeared after the lift [10].

KA-9: Congress formally reviews the event: almost certain. Officials will likely press for $30-50 billion in air traffic control funding: moderate confidence.
Moderate confidence (funding)

The House committee has committed to a review [20]; both Secretaries called for large funding increases the same night [27, 25].

09 · What to Watch, 24 to 72 Hours

  • An FAA or DOT written root-cause statement, and whether it confirms the update-and-reboot account.
  • CISA or FBI statements on the cyber question. A change from "zero evidence" would be significant.
  • DOT Inspector General or congressional hearings and letters; the funding request.
  • Any White House document confirming a "national emergency" declaration (currently single-source).
  • Recovery: Saturday cancellation counts, carrier-specific stops, crew duty-time limits.
  • The status of the SMART tool, for example any pause or review.
  • Speculation drift: cyber or Iran narratives if no written cause appears; named-company blame; attempts to link the outage to the New Orleans collision.

10 · Actions for Organizations with Travel or Aviation Exposure

  1. Assume knock-on cancellations through November 15. Rebook critical travel early using the change waivers Delta, American, and United announced [21, 22, 24]. Pre-book ground alternatives for short trips.
  2. Time-critical shipments: confirm carrier contingency status [25].
  3. Thanksgiving travel (November 26): add buffer days. Officials expect normal operations by then, but the cause is not yet determined.
  4. Aviation operators: re-brief lost-communications and diversion procedures. Monitor FAA command center advisories and the NAS Status page directly, not social media.
  5. Security and communications teams: treat cyber attribution claims as unverified, avoid repeating company names no official source has named, and, as a precaution rather than an observed threat, warn staff about rebooking- and refund-themed phishing.
