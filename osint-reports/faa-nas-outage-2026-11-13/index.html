Flash Briefing: FAA Nationwide Ground Stop

United States National Airspace System · Friday, November 13, 2026 · Flash Briefing v2, November 14, 2026

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) halted departures at every U.S. airport for about four hours and 22 to 23 minutes after a failure in its ground telecommunications network hit multiple air route traffic control centers. On Saturday, officials said on the record that a router at a remote FAA site, where a contractor was moving lines from copper to fiber, began flooding other routers. Whether contractor error or faulty equipment was responsible is not yet determined, and a root-cause analysis is pending. Officials say they "don't believe this was cyber at all."

Bottom Line Up Front

  • The event is confirmed and closed. The FAA command center ordered a national ground stop of all departing traffic, effective 3:53 PM ET (20:53 UTC) on Friday, November 13 [2]. Officials said it was lifted at 8:15 PM ET (01:15 UTC) [15]; the command center's cancellation advisory runs from 01:16 UTC and was signed at 8:19 PM ET [54].
  • "All flights grounded" remains overstated. The order stopped departures. More than 3,500 airborne aircraft landed, and exempt and some international flights continued [2, 15, 23, 25].
  • Official cause account (on record Saturday; not yet a written finding): during contractor work at a remote FAA site to move telecommunication lines from copper to fiber, a router "started flooding other routers" with "over 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds", disrupting communications with aircraft and radar feeds. Officials have not determined whether contractor error or faulty equipment was responsible; a root-cause analysis is pending [32].
  • Conflicts remain between that account and earlier reporting of a software update or patch [28, 86]. The type of change that started the failure is unresolved.
  • Cyberattack: unlikely (moderate confidence). Officials now say "we don't believe this was cyber at all" [32]. No CISA or FBI public statement was found on the pages checked [64-68].
  • Recovery: 4,269 U.S. cancellations on Saturday by 12:05 PM ET [41]. The FAA expects full recovery by the end of Sunday, November 15 [75].

01 · What the Order Did, and What It Did Not

The official order

Command center Advisory 111 (signed 3:54 PM ET / 20:54 UTC): "ALL AIRPORTS ARE GROUND STOPPED. ALL DEPARTING TRAFFIC SHOULD REMAIN GROUND STOPPED UNTIL FURTHER NOTICE. INBOUND INTERNATIONAL FLIGHTS WILL BE MANAGED ON A CASE BY CASE BASIS." [2]

What continued during the stop

  • Airborne aircraft continued: more than 3,500 airborne at the stop, fewer than 400 by 8:00 PM ET (01:00 UTC) [15].
  • Exempt flights: medevac, life-saving cargo, and law-enforcement flights [15].
  • Alaska and Hawaii: flights within Alaska and Hawaii inter-island flights were not affected [23].
  • International: some departures to Europe and across the Pacific were released [25, 26, 36]. At 7:37 PM ET (00:37 UTC) the command center said the stop "REMAINS IN EFFECT FOR A LARGE PORTION OF THE COUNTRY", with some coastal facilities releasing some international departures [55].

03 · Official Statements

Saturday briefing, November 14 (Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy; FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford)
  • Duffy: "In at least one of our remote FAA locations, a contractor was doing routine maintenance to switch telecommunication lines from old copper wire, and they were bringing it over to fiber." [32]
  • Duffy: "Either through contractor error or a faulty piece of equipment, that router that was being replaced as we switched from ... copper to fiber, it started flooding other routers in our air traffic control system. So specifically, we had over 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds" [32].
  • Duffy: "Once we were able to isolate the work that was being done at this remote location, all of our systems came back online, so we don't believe this was cyber at all." [32]
  • Bedford: "We see zero evidence of anything nefarious, and zero evidence of anything AI-related. So we want to be clear that we have a equipment issue" [32]. "When we have that root cause analysis complete, we'll share it with you." [32]
  • Duffy, on SMART: "This is a system that's not tied into or connected to the systems that went down yesterday." [78]
  • An FAA technology official said the FAA disabled the problem function on the roughly 60 routers of the same type in use nationwide (Yahoo News, paraphrase) [75]. Bedford said the legacy network "will continue to break until we replace it" [75].
  • Officials named the network contractor at the briefing [76]. This briefing does not name it, pending the root-cause analysis.
  • Duffy will send Congress a new funding request on Monday, November 16 [75].
FAA command center
  • Advisory 120: "REASON: COMMUNICATIONS FAILURE" [3]. Advisory 117: drop times extended "DUE TO NATIONWIDE COMMS FAILURE" [61]. Advisory 005 cancelled the national stop; its reason field is blank [54].
  • No written FAA or DOT root-cause statement was read: faa.gov and transportation.gov returned HTTP 403 to automated access at 10:54-10:55 AM ET and about 12:04 PM ET.

04 · Credible Reporting Beyond Official Statements

CBS News, anonymous senior government sources
  • Friday: an update to the private network connecting the air traffic control system caused it to reboot, crash, and lose communications [28].
  • Saturday: the FAA's preliminary investigation led it to believe the issue was limited to routers of a specific older model line (2001-2006 manufacture); the routers "essentially got overwhelmed and started feeding out-of-sync data packets" into the wider system; the faulty equipment has been identified and isolated [28]. We do not name the router vendor, which rests only on anonymous sources.
Bloomberg, one unnamed person (relays only)

Relayed by SecurityWeek and Al Jazeera: the FBI does not think a cyberattack or criminal activity was behind the outage, and investigators are examining whether a software update caused it [86, 89]. The Bloomberg article itself could not be read. This is not an FBI statement; none was found on the FBI pages checked.

How the accounts compare
  • Established by two or more independent sources: a failure in the FAA's ground telecommunications network [61, 3, 32, 28]; restoration by isolating the faulty element [32, 28].
  • On record from one press conference only (relayed by several outlets): copper-to-fiber work at a remote site, a flooding router, and more than 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds [32, 75, 82, 83].
  • Conflicts: a software update or patch (CBS Friday; Bloomberg relays) versus physical line work with a flooding router (Duffy, Saturday); reboot and crash versus overload by connection flooding [28, 86, 32]. No source establishes a single chain from change to flood to overload.
Cascade (The Air Current, single source)

Outages began at the Chicago en route center, with Boston, Washington, New York, and Minneapolis "verifying they had problems as well in rapid succession"; a carrier dispatcher was told 10 en route centers were affected "and growing" [36].

06 · Key Assessments

KA-1: Departures were halted nationwide from 3:53 PM ET to 8:15-8:19 PM ET. Almost certain; high confidence.
High confidence

Advisory 111 and its cancellation, Advisory 005 [2, 54]; officials [15]; more than ten outlets.

KA-2: A failure in the FAA's ground telecommunications network affecting multiple en route centers was the proximate trigger. Almost certain; high confidence on the category (raised from very likely).
High confidence

New evidence: Advisory 117 ("NATIONWIDE COMMS FAILURE") [61] and the Saturday on-record account [32]. Still unresolved: whether all centers were affected [12, 1, 36].

KA-3: The disruption began in the Chicago area before the 3:36 PM ET onset officials cited. Likely; moderate confidence (unchanged).
Moderate confidence

FAA advisories for O'Hare ("COMMS ISSUES" from 3:10 PM ET) and Midway [5, 6], plus The Air Current [36].

KA-4: A change to the network started the failure. Likely; moderate confidence.
Moderate confidence

The type of change (physical line migration, router replacement, or a software update) is unresolved [32, 28, 86]. Whether contractor error or faulty equipment was responsible is undetermined, as officials say [32]. No written root-cause finding has been read.

KA-5: A cyberattack caused the outage. Unlikely; moderate confidence (unchanged).
Moderate confidence

On-record statements from three senior officials [32, 17]; relayed reporting on the FBI's view [86]. Limits: no CISA or FBI public statement found; FBI checks partial; root-cause analysis pending.

KA-6: The SMART AI tool caused the outage. Unlikely; moderate confidence (raised from low).
Moderate confidence

Officials say SMART is not connected to the systems that failed, and the mechanism account points to the telecom layer [78, 32]. Limits: no FAA written finding; the developer's denial comes from an interested party.

KA-7: The outage is linked to the Microsoft outage. Very unlikely; moderate confidence (unchanged).
Moderate confidence

Microsoft says it has "no indication" of a link; the DHS Secretary and officials say it was not linked [25, 27, 26]. The Saturday account describes a different mechanism [32].

KA-8: Flight disruption continues into Sunday, November 15. Very likely; moderate confidence.
Moderate confidence

4,269 U.S. cancellations by midday Saturday [41]; the FAA expects full recovery by the end of Sunday [75]; carriers report lingering effects [22].

KA-9: "National emergency" rests on a statement, not a formal declaration found. No confidence rating assigned.

The DHS release carries the line [63]; no presidential document or Federal Register notice was found [71-74]. The Federal Register check is weak until Monday, November 16.
