2026 US Cyclosporiasis Outbreak

Multistate · Reporting period from May 1, 2026 · Briefing v4, July 18, 2026 (data as of Jul 17)

Open-source threat-intelligence briefing on the largest domestic Cyclospora cayetanensis event on record. A parasite spread through the fresh-produce supply chain has produced 1,645 CDC-confirmed domestic cases across 34 states, with more than 5,100 under analysis and state-reported totals above 7,500, far exceeding the ~4,700-case US record set in 2019. FDA and CDC now officially link a five-state Taco Bell cluster (1,644 cases / 94 hospitalized) to iceberg lettuce sourced from Taylor Farms de Mexico, and Taylor Farms has begun a voluntary recall. That attribution covers only part of the outbreak: North Carolina's health department says its cases are a separate, unlinked source. CDC's national confirmed total has not moved in five days. We assess the outbreak as a real-world stress test of degraded US biosurveillance and food-safety trace-back capacity.

Bottom Line Up Front

This is a clinically mild but epidemiologically significant outbreak. Severity is low: the illness is self-limiting, treatable with antibiotics, and not spread person-to-person. The strategic signal is elsewhere, and it moved from lead to finding this week. FDA and CDC now officially link a five-state Taco Bell cluster to Taylor Farms de Mexico iceberg lettuce, and Taylor Farms has begun a voluntary recall, ending the anonymous-sourcing problem we flagged in v3. But the attribution does not close the outbreak: North Carolina's health department says its own cases are not linked to the Taco Bell cluster, a second vehicle we had flagged only as a live possibility. Meanwhile CDC's national confirmed count has sat frozen at 1,645 for five straight days even as agencies published a fresh, current count for the Taco Bell cluster alone, which weakens the "latency, not concealment" reading we gave that freeze in v3. Together these point the same direction as our original thesis: the July 2025 reduction of CDC's FoodNet active surveillance from eight pathogens to two (Cyclospora among those dropped), plus an approximately 25% workforce cut, have left a system that can still count cases and can still run a traceback, but is visibly uneven in what it discloses and when.

What changed since v3 (Jul 16 → Jul 17). FDA and CDC published official outbreak pages linking a five-state Taco Bell cluster (IN, KY, MI, OH, WV, 1,644 cases / 94 hospitalized as of Jul 17) to shredded iceberg lettuce sourced from Taylor Farms de Mexico. An FDA consumer advisory for those five states is now published, matching the reported scope exactly. Taylor Farms broke its silence with a public statement, is voluntarily withdrawing all central-Mexico iceberg lettuce, and has told FDA it will initiate a recall; it does not admit fault and says no Taylor Farms-branded salads or kits are implicated. Michigan climbed to 5,002 cases / 102 hospitalized (from 4,312 the prior day). CDC's national confirmed count did not move: still 1,645 / 141 hospitalized / 0 deaths, unchanged since Jul 13, now a five-day freeze.

Two notes. First, the FDA outbreak page and the Taylor Farms statement were retrieved directly and their wording verified for this update: FDA states its traceback "identified convergence on a single supplier, Taylor Farms de Mexico," publishes the 1,644 / 94 / 0 case figures, and advises against Taylor Farms de Mexico shredded iceberg lettuce at Taco Bell in the five states; the CDC outbreak page corroborates the same figures and naming. Second, and more important: North Carolina's health department states its 307 cases (Wake County-concentrated) are not part of the Taco Bell cluster, confirming the second-vehicle possibility we flagged in v3. A five-state restaurant-linked finding still does not explain a 34-state outbreak.

Figures are time-sensitive and stratified. The CDC-confirmed count (1,645), the under-analysis pool (>5,100), and the raw state aggregate (>7,500) measure different things; conflating them fuels a false "CDC undercount/cover-up" narrative. That said, the gap is now wide enough to warrant harder scrutiny than we gave it in v3: CDC's national figure has not moved since Jul 13, a five-day freeze, while Michigan alone has added 690 cases in a single day and agencies separately published a fresh, current count for the Taco Bell cluster. That last point cuts against a pure "batch latency" explanation: the agencies can and did publish a current number this period, just not the topline one. We still read the freeze as adjudication backlog rather than concealment, but with lower confidence than in v3. See the Full Briefing for the reconciliation.

Predictive Assessments

Predictions are time-stamped at publication and tracked for calibration. Resolution status is updated as confirming or disconfirming evidence emerges, including when the evidence cuts against us. Baseline date: July 13, 2026 (v1); updated July 15, 2026 (v2), July 16, 2026 (v3), and July 18, 2026 (v4).
Prediction 1: A leafy-green or fresh-herb vehicle

If a food vehicle is ever identified, it will be a leafy-green/salad blend or a fresh herb (basil/cilantro), consistent with every resolved US Cyclospora outbreak since 2013, not an exotic new commodity.

v2 update (Jul 15): Trending toward confirmation. Michigan officials now cite lettuce or salad greens as the probable vehicle and FDA is investigating produce, consistent with this prediction, though no vehicle is officially confirmed. Confidence raised 70% to 80%.

v3 update (Jul 16): Effectively confirmed pending official word. The reported traceback lands on iceberg lettuce, squarely inside the predicted commodity class, and on a Mexican-grown import, matching the second half of the base rate. Confidence raised 80% to 90%. We withhold full resolution only because no agency has confirmed on the record.

v4 update (Jul 17): Confirmed. FDA and CDC now officially link the five-state Taco Bell cluster to iceberg lettuce. North Carolina's suspected (unconfirmed) foods for its separate cluster, parsley, cilantro, and lettuce, also sit inside this predicted commodity class. Confidence raised 90% to 95%.

Prediction 2: Source likely never conclusively named

Given the long incubation window, produce commingling, perishable evidence, and stressed trace-back capacity, there is a better-than-even chance this outbreak closes without a single conclusively identified food source.

v3 update (Jul 16): trending against us. FDA's traceback reportedly reached a named supplier, commodity, and country of origin inside roughly ten weeks. That is a materially better outcome than we forecast, and we are marking the confidence down 60% to 40%. Two reasons we are not abandoning the call outright: the attribution is not yet on the record, and a five-state restaurant cluster may account for only part of a 34-state outbreak, leaving the remainder unattributed even if Taylor Farms is formally named. The 2013 precedent is instructive here: FDA named Taylor Farms de Mexico and still concluded that not all cases were related to each other.

v4 update (Jul 17): disconfirmed for the named cluster, open for the outbreak as a whole. FDA and CDC named a supplier, commodity, and country of origin on the record within about eleven weeks, for the Taco Bell cluster that call is now clearly wrong. We are splitting the resolution: disconfirmed for that cluster, but North Carolina's separate, still-unattributed cases show a real share of the 34-state case load remains without a named source, exactly the mechanism this prediction anticipated. Lowering overall confidence 40% to 25%, reflecting that the prediction's narrow form failed while its broader spirit is holding for part of the outbreak.

Prediction 3: Confirmed count converges upward toward state tallies

As the under-analysis pool is adjudicated, the CDC-confirmed figure will rise substantially above the v1 figure of 843, narrowing (but not closing) the gap to the state aggregate, validating that the true burden sits well above the confirmed count.

v2 update (Jul 15): Confirmed. CDC-confirmed cases roughly doubled to 1,645 in two days and the under-analysis pool grew to >5,100 (nearly 7,000 combined), exceeding the ~4,700-case 2019 US record.

v3 correction (Jul 16): we called this too early. The prediction had two halves. The count did rise sharply (843 to 1,645), which v2 marked as confirmation. But the second half, narrowing the gap, has not happened and has in fact reversed: CDC has been static at 1,645 since Jul 13 while the state aggregate climbed past 7,500, so the gap widened from roughly 2.4x to 4.6x. Marking this back down from Confirmed to Partially confirmed and reducing confidence 80% to 55%. The underlying claim (true burden far exceeds the confirmed count) is holding; our model of how the numbers would reconcile was wrong. Adjudication appears to be a batch process with multi-day latency, not the continuous convergence we assumed.

v4 update (Jul 17): the gap kept widening, and our own charitable explanation looks weaker. CDC's national count is still static at 1,645, a five-day freeze, while Michigan alone is now above 5,000 and the state aggregate sits well past 7,500. More importantly, agencies published a fresh, current, cluster-specific count (1,644/94) in the same period they left the national topline untouched, which undercuts the pure "batch latency" reading we gave this in v3. We are lowering confidence further, 55% to 40%, and flagging this as the item most deserving of harder scrutiny next cycle.

Prediction 4: Surveillance-capacity becomes the durable story

Independent of the clinical outcome, the lasting policy narrative will be the FoodNet reduction and CDC workforce cuts as a case study in eroded outbreak-response readiness, cited in future foodborne-illness and public-health-funding debates.

v3 update (Jul 16): Strengthened. Two developments feed this narrative directly: CDC's confirmed count stalling for three days while state totals surged, and the supplier lead reaching the public through a leaked source to reporters rather than an FDA advisory. Whatever the eventual attribution, "the press named the supplier before the agency did" is a durable talking point. Confidence raised 75% to 80%.

v4 update (Jul 17): Reinforced further. The advisory and recall arrived only after five days of a frozen national count and mounting press pressure, and a fresh cluster-specific figure landed before the topline did. Confidence raised 80% to 83%.

Prediction 5: Attribution will not close the case (new in v3)

Even if FDA formally names Taylor Farms iceberg lettuce, a substantial share of the 34-state case load will remain unattributed to that vehicle. The reported cluster spans five states and one restaurant chain; the outbreak does not. We expect the final picture to include either much broader distribution of the same supplier's product beyond Taco Bell, or a second concurrent vehicle. The 2013 analogue closed exactly this way, with FDA noting that not all cases were directly related.

v4 update (Jul 17): trending confirmed. North Carolina's health department states its 307 cases are not part of the Taco Bell cluster, direct evidence of exactly the second-vehicle mechanism this prediction anticipated, one week after we wrote it. FDA's own attribution remains scoped to five states. Confidence raised 70% to 85%.

Prediction 6: Litigation outruns attribution (new in v3)

Civil suits against Taco Bell franchisees, Taco Bell corporate, and Taylor Farms will accumulate faster than the regulatory record supports, with the first filed (Ayyad v.
