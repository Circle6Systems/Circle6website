2026 US Cyclosporiasis Outbreak

Multistate · Reporting period from May 1, 2026 · Briefing v10, July 23, 2026 (data as of Jul 22)

Open-source threat-intelligence briefing on the largest domestic Cyclospora cayetanensis event on record. A parasite spread through the fresh-produce supply chain has produced 4,173 CDC-confirmed domestic cases across 41 states as of Jul 22, up sharply from 1,645/34 states, where the confirmed count had sat frozen for nine days. CDC also reports more than 7,400 additional cases under analysis, a combined total above 11,500, far exceeding the ~4,700-case US record set in 2019. FDA and CDC officially link a five-state Taco Bell cluster (1,644 cases / 94 hospitalized) to iceberg lettuce sourced from Taylor Farms de Mexico. A lettuce sample from FDA's own import surveillance briefly tested positive for Cyclospora, which would have been the strongest evidence in the case to date, but FDA retracted the result the next day as a false positive; the agency's epidemiological and traceback conclusion stands, but the attribution again rests on that convergence rather than a confirmed contaminated sample. Taylor Farms' recall spans 27 states, including retail bagged lettuce sold at Walmart, far broader than the five-state cluster FDA's advisory covers. North Carolina sits on that 27-state distribution list even though its health department maintains its own cases are a separate, unlinked source, a tension this briefing has not resolved. We assess the outbreak as a real-world stress test of degraded US biosurveillance and food-safety trace-back capacity.

Bottom Line Up Front

This is a clinically mild but epidemiologically significant outbreak. Severity is low: the illness is self-limiting, treatable with antibiotics, and not spread person-to-person. The attribution to Taylor Farms de Mexico briefly gained a materially different kind of evidence this cycle: FDA disclosed that its own targeted import surveillance had returned a direct positive laboratory test for Cyclospora on a Taylor Farms lettuce sample, not just epidemiologic convergence on a supplier. One day later, FDA reversed itself, calling the result a false positive after re-review and reportedly apologizing to Taylor Farms. FDA states its epidemiological and traceback conclusion is unchanged, so the attribution for the named cluster still clears our bar for a finding, but the case's one piece of direct physical evidence has now been withdrawn, and we say so plainly rather than let the earlier, stronger framing stand uncorrected. Taylor Farms' recall notice still covers 27 states and retail bagged product, not just the five-state restaurant cluster. That is not the same claim as "the outbreak is solved." North Carolina's health department still says its own cases are not linked to the Taco Bell cluster, yet North Carolina is itself on the 27-state recall distribution list, an overlap we flag rather than resolve. Multiple law firms have now filed suits naming Taylor Farms directly, not just Taco Bell. CDC's national confirmed count, frozen at 1,645 for nine days, jumped to 4,173 across 41 states on Jul 22 as the agency processed its adjudication backlog. Together these continue to point toward our original thesis: the July 2025 reduction of CDC's FoodNet active surveillance from eight pathogens to two (Cyclospora among those dropped), plus an approximately 25% workforce cut, have left a system that can still count cases and can still run a traceback, but is visibly uneven in what it discloses and when, and whose own lab results are not yet fully reliable.

What changed in v10 (Jul 22): the CDC freeze broke, ending the outbreak's single longest-running open question. CDC's national confirmed count, static at 1,645 since Jul 13, jumped to 4,173 confirmed cases across 41 states on Jul 22, with more than 7,400 additional cases still under analysis, a combined total above 11,500. Per the Washington Post, the agency attributes the gap to its own adjudication method: CDC counts only cases states have formally reported as lab-confirmed and assumes roughly a six-week reporting lag, while states such as Michigan count confirmed and probable cases close to real time, so state totals run higher and climb faster. CDC also reported a national hospitalization figure of 308 tied to the new confirmed total, up from 141. This is a direct, on-the-record resolution of the question Predictions 3 and 4 have tracked since v3, and we treat it as such below.

State totals kept climbing too. Michigan rose to 7,171 cases as of Jul 22 (MDHHS), up from 6,571 the day before, a 9% single-day increase. North Carolina's total reached 561 cases as of Jul 21, up from more than 500, with hospitalizations rising to 16; NC DHHS continues to maintain these cases are unlinked to the Taco Bell cluster.
