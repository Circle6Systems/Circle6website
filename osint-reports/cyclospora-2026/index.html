2026 US Cyclosporiasis Outbreak

Multistate · Reporting period from May 1, 2026 · Briefing v25, August 10, 2026 (data as of Aug 10)

Open-source threat-intelligence briefing on the largest domestic Cyclospora cayetanensis event on record. A parasite spread through the fresh-produce supply chain has produced 10,468 CDC-confirmed domestic cases across 47 states (that national surveillance figure, data through Aug 3, is unchanged this cycle, with CDC's next weekly update expected around Aug 11), far exceeding the ~4,700-case US record set in 2019.

On Aug 5 FDA and CDC expanded the Taco Bell / Taylor Farms de Mexico iceberg-lettuce cluster from nine states to 15, adding Arkansas, Iowa, Missouri, Nebraska, New Hampshire and North Carolina; the cluster now stands at 6,358 confirmed cases, 278 hospitalizations and 2 deaths (both in Michigan), up from 1,947 / 98 / 0. The jump comes from broadening the case definition to include people who reported exposure to either Taco Bell or recalled Taylor Farms de Mexico iceberg lettuce, plus the six new states.

The lettuce attribution is the one Mexico's own health ministry has publicly disputed on the record, saying its investigation found no evidence the outbreak originated there; Mexican authorities separately report that lettuce and water samples from the implicated Guanajuato facility tested negative for Cyclospora and fecal coliforms. Taylor Farms has temporarily, voluntarily shut the implicated Mexico processing facility even though no product sample from that facility has ever tested positive. FDA separately discloses two ongoing additional outbreaks with products still "not yet identified," not linked to the lettuce cluster. Taylor Farms' recall spans 27 states, including retail bagged lettuce sold at Walmart.

North Carolina is now federally counted inside the cluster, but the state's own health department has not followed: NCDHHS's page, last updated Aug 4, still says its cases do not appear associated with the Taco Bell outbreak and points instead to parsley and cilantro. We surface that state-versus-federal disagreement rather than resolve it, and assess the outbreak as a real-world stress test of degraded US biosurveillance and food-safety trace-back capacity.

Bottom Line Up Front

A generally mild illness, now the largest domestic Cyclospora event on record, and, as of this cycle, a fatal one. For most people cyclosporiasis is self-limiting, treatable with antibiotics, and not spread person-to-person. But the scale is unprecedented and the outbreak is no longer fatality-free.

  • The national count jumped again. CDC's Aug 4 weekly update reads 10,468 confirmed cases across 47 states (517 hospitalized), up from 6,707/45 the prior week, the third catch-up jump since the Jul 22 unfreeze. CDC separately counts more than 12,255 additional cases not yet lab-confirmed, many from Michigan, putting the confirmed-plus-under-analysis total above 22,700. All figures re-fetched directly from CDC this cycle.
  • CDC now confirms the 2 deaths. Last cycle Michigan MDHHS reported the outbreak's first two deaths while CDC's national page still read 0, a reporting lag, as this briefing said. That lag has now closed: CDC's Aug 4 page reads 2 deaths. MDHHS notes both decedents "had significant underlying health conditions" and both became ill before the July 17 recall; the illness is still "generally not life-threatening," but it is no longer accurate to call this outbreak fatality-free.
  • The Mexico attribution is under government-level dispute. Already weakened by a retracted lab result, the Taylor Farms de Mexico traceback took a direct hit: Mexico's Health Secretary said on the record that its investigation found no evidence the outbreak originated there, and Mexican authorities report lettuce and water samples from the implicated Guanajuato facility tested negative for Cyclospora and fecal coliforms. FDA and CDC have not withdrawn their conclusion, so the attribution still clears our bar, but a named foreign government publicly disputing a named US agency's traceback is new, and the "High confidence" badge no longer stands unqualified.
  • The named cluster more than tripled and now spans 15 states. On Aug 5 FDA and CDC expanded the Taco Bell / Taylor Farms de Mexico lettuce cluster from nine states to 15 (adding AR, IA, MO, NE, NH and NC), at 6,358 confirmed cases, 278 hospitalizations and 2 deaths, up from 1,947 / 98 / 0. FDA attributes the jump to broadening the case definition to include exposure to either Taco Bell or recalled Taylor Farms lettuce, plus the six new states; many of the added illnesses began before the July 17 recall. Taylor Farms' recall still spans 27 states of retail bagged product, and FDA separately discloses two ongoing outbreaks whose products remain "not yet identified." None of this means the outbreak is solved.
  • North Carolina is where the federal and state accounts now openly disagree. FDA and CDC added NC to the 15-state cluster on Aug 5 on the strength of lab/strain typing. But NCDHHS's own page, last updated Aug 4 (the day before), still reads 867 / 33 and still says the increase in NC cases "does not seem to be associated with the outbreak in other states involving Taco Bell," pointing instead to parsley and cilantro. NCDHHS refreshes on Tuesdays, so its Aug 11 update may reconcile the two; until then we carry the disagreement openly rather than adopt the federal framing over the state's own words.

Why it matters. Together these point to our standing thesis: the July 2025 reduction of CDC's FoodNet active surveillance from eight pathogens to two (Cyclospora among those dropped), plus an ~25% workforce cut, have left a system that can still count cases and run a traceback, but only in periodic batches rather than continuously, is uneven in what it discloses and when, whose own lab results are not yet fully reliable, and whose lead foreign-supplier attribution a source country's own government now disputes with evidence of its own.
