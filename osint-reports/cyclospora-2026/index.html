2026 US Cyclosporiasis Outbreak

Multistate · Reporting period from May 1, 2026 · Briefing v13, July 27, 2026 (data as of Jul 26)

Open-source threat-intelligence briefing on the largest domestic Cyclospora cayetanensis event on record. A parasite spread through the fresh-produce supply chain has produced 4,173 CDC-confirmed domestic cases across 41 states (no further national movement found since Jul 22, now a second consecutive quiet cycle), far exceeding the ~4,700-case US record set in 2019. FDA and CDC officially link a nine-state Taco Bell cluster (1,947 cases / at least 98 hospitalized), expanded from five states on Jul 24, to iceberg lettuce sourced from Taylor Farms de Mexico, an attribution that Mexico's own health ministry has now publicly disputed on the record, saying its investigation found no evidence the outbreak originated there; Mexican authorities separately report that lettuce and water samples from the implicated Guanajuato facility tested negative for Cyclospora and fecal coliforms. Taylor Farms has temporarily, voluntarily shut the implicated Mexico processing facility even though no product sample from that facility has ever tested positive. FDA separately disclosed a distinct, additional outbreak of at least 72 cases with a product still "not yet identified," not linked to the lettuce cluster. Taylor Farms' recall spans 27 states, including retail bagged lettuce sold at Walmart, broader than the nine-state cluster FDA's advisory now covers. North Carolina sits on that 27-state distribution list even though its health department maintains its own cases are a separate, unlinked source, a tension this briefing has not resolved. We assess the outbreak as a real-world stress test of degraded US biosurveillance and food-safety trace-back capacity.

Bottom Line Up Front

This is a clinically mild but epidemiologically significant outbreak. Severity is low: the illness is self-limiting, treatable with antibiotics, and not spread person-to-person. The Taylor Farms de Mexico attribution, already weakened by a retracted lab result, took a second, more direct hit this cycle: Mexico's own Health Secretary said on the record that his country's investigation found no evidence the outbreak originated there, a named foreign government publicly disputing the named US agencies' traceback conclusion. FDA and CDC have not withdrawn that conclusion, so the attribution for the named cluster still clears our bar for a finding, but a government-level dispute is new and we say so plainly rather than let the "High confidence" badge on that attribution stand unqualified. Separately, Taylor Farms voluntarily and temporarily closed its implicated central-Mexico processing facility even though no product sample from that facility has ever tested positive, and FDA disclosed a wholly separate outbreak of at least 72 cases whose product is still "not yet identified." Taylor Farms' recall notice still covers 27 states and retail bagged product, wider than the named restaurant cluster, which itself grew this cycle: CDC retitled its outbreak page from a five-state to a nine-state outbreak on Jul 24, adding Illinois, Kansas, Oklahoma, and Pennsylvania, with the cluster-specific count rising to 1,947 cases and at least 98 hospitalized. That is not the same claim as "the outbreak is solved." North Carolina's health department still says its own cases are not linked to the Taco Bell cluster, yet North Carolina is itself on the 27-state recall distribution list, an overlap we flag rather than resolve. CDC's national confirmed count, which broke a nine-day freeze on Jul 22 at 4,173 across 41 states, has not moved further across two consecutive cycles now. Mexico's dispute also firmed up this cycle: Mexican authorities report lettuce and water samples from the implicated Guanajuato facility tested negative for Cyclospora and fecal coliforms, though officials caution a negative plant test does not by itself resolve where in the supply chain contamination occurred, and FDA has not withdrawn its own conclusion. Together these continue to point toward our original thesis: the July 2025 reduction of CDC's FoodNet active surveillance from eight pathogens to two (Cyclospora among those dropped), plus an approximately 25% workforce cut, have left a system that can still count cases and can still run a traceback, but is visibly uneven in what it discloses and when, whose own lab results are not yet fully reliable, and whose lead foreign-supplier attribution a source country's own government now disputes with an evidentiary result of its own.

A second, distinct outbreak. FDA disclosed it is investigating a separate cyclosporiasis outbreak of at least 72 cases with a product, brand, or supplier "not yet identified," explicitly not linked to the Taylor Farms lettuce cluster. Coverage of how many total cyclospora investigations FDA has open varies by outlet and by date (from "at least four" to the five clusters, three closed, disclosed in v9), so we do not assert a specific running total; what is confirmed is that this specific 72-case cluster is new and distinct. Also this cycle, the CDC outbreak-page retitling this briefing has carried as an unconfirmed lead since v9 is now search-confirmed: CDC's own page is titled "Cyclospora Outbreak Linked to Iceberg Lettuce in 5 States" and a companion investigation-update page names Taylor Farms de Mexico verbatim. This closes our single most overdue watch item, though we still label it search-indexed rather than primary-verified.

What changed in v10 (Jul 22): the CDC freeze broke, ending the outbreak's single longest-running open question. CDC's national confirmed count, static at 1,645 since Jul 13, jumped to 4,173 confirmed cases across 41 states on Jul 22, with more than 7,400 additional cases still under analysis, a combined total above 11,500. Per the Washington Post, the agency attributes the gap to its own adjudication method: CDC counts only cases states have formally reported as lab-confirmed and assumes roughly a six-week reporting lag, while states such as Michigan count confirmed and probable cases close to real time, so state totals run higher and climb faster. CDC also reported a national hospitalization figure of 308 tied to the new confirmed total, up from 141. This is a direct, on-the-record resolution of the question Predictions 3 and 4 have tracked since v3, and we treat it as such below.

North Carolina's total reached 561 cases as of Jul 21, up from more than 500, with hospitalizations rising to 16; NC DHHS continues to maintain these cases are unlinked to the Taco Bell cluster.

What changed since v5 (Jul 18 → Jul 19). FDA disclosed that its own targeted import surveillance collected a sample of Taylor Farms de Mexico shredded iceberg lettuce at the border that tested positive for Cyclospora, a direct laboratory finding rather than an epidemiologic traceback inference. FDA states the positive lot was detained and is not part of Taylor Farms' currently posted 27-state recall; the company says it is working to determine whether any of that lot reached commerce or consumers' homes. No source we found discloses the lot number, ship dates, or which states or customers that lot was destined for, a gap Marler Blog's coverage criticized directly. This is the strongest form of evidence for the Taylor Farms attribution to date, but it also raises a live, unanswered question: whether the current 27-state recall fully captures the contaminated product, or whether this positive lot represents a gap in it.

Still unresolved from v5. Taylor Farms' own recall notice, distributed Jun 29 through Jul 16 to 27 states (AL, AR, CT, FL, GA, IA, IL, IN, KS, KY, LA, MA, MD, MI, MO, MS, NC, NH, NJ, OH, OK, PA, SC, TN, TX, VA, WI), including retail bagged product (Walmart's Marketside Bagged Iceberg Salad and Shredded Iceberg Salad), still stands as previously reported. FDA states its epidemiological and traceback conclusion, that the outbreak is linked to Taylor Farms de Mexico iceberg lettuce, is unchanged, so under our own hedge-discipline standard this remains a finding rather than a demoted lead. But v6's specific claim that this was now the strongest evidence in the case no longer holds, and we are correcting it here rather than quietly dropping it. We are reverting Prediction 1's v6 confidence bump (97% back to 95%), since it was based specifically on this now-retracted lab result, and revising Prediction 4 to reflect that the "capability-positive" reading v6 gave this detection no longer applies either, since the detection itself was erroneous. Everything else carried forward from v6 (the 27-state recall, the North Carolina/recall overlap, litigation naming Taylor Farms entities directly) is unaffected by this correction and stands as previously reported; we found no source this cycle that changes any of it. Prediction 1: A leafy-green or fresh-herb vehicle

If a food vehicle is ever identified, it will be a leafy-green/salad blend or a fresh herb (basil/cilantro), consistent with every resolved US Cyclospora outbreak since 2013, not an exotic new commodity.

The reported traceback lands on iceberg lettuce, squarely inside the predicted commodity class, and on a Mexican-grown import, matching the second half of the base rate. Confidence raised 80% to 90%. v5 update (Jul 18): No new information this cycle bears on the commodity class itself. Taylor Farms' newly published recall covers the same commodity (iceberg lettuce), reinforcing rather than changing this call. Holding at 95%.
